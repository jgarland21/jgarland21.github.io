About Me

I am a PhD candidate in the David A. Dunlap Department of Astronomy & Astrophysics at the University of Toronto, where I work with Professor Laurie Rousseau-Nepton and the SIGNALS collaboration. My research focuses on how spiral structure stirs and enriches the gas in nearby galaxies: I map fine-scale variations in chemical abundance across spiral arms, and I am leading a CFHT/SITELLE program to study dust extinction in the HII regions of M33.

Before coming to Toronto, I spent a year as a research assistant at the American Museum of Natural History with Dr. Michael Shara, using the Condor Array Telescope to search for extragalactic novae and the faint "super-remnants" that surround recurrent novae. I graduated from Haverford College in 2022 with a B.S. in physics and astronomy, where I worked with Professors Karen Masters and Daniel Grin on tidally induced bars in galaxies and on observational tests of axion dark matter. You can read more about all of this on my research page.

Science communication has been a constant alongside my research. I have been heavily involved in U of T's AstroTours public outreach program, including a year as co-director, I have taught tutorials and labs for U of T astronomy courses, and as an undergraduate I co-led public observing nights at Haverford's Strawbridge Observatory.

Outside of academia, I am an avid birder and bird photographer; you can find my photos on Instagram at @jameslikesbirds. I also enjoy playing guitar, and in the past I made jewelry (a selection of that work can be found here).

You can find my CV here. If you would like to reach me, send me an email and I'll get back to you as soon as possible.
