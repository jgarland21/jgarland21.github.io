Research

I am an observational astronomer. My current work focuses on how spiral structure stirs and enriches the gas in nearby galaxies, and I have previously worked on novae and their remnants, the tidal evolution of galaxies, and observational tests of dark matter. Most broadly, I am interested in observational astronomy both for its own discoveries and as a test of computational and theoretical predictions.

For a more comprehensive review of my work and experience, please see my CV. A full list of my publications is below and on ORCID.

Current Work

Spiral Arms and Chemical Enrichment in Nearby Galaxies

At the University of Toronto, I work with Professor Laurie Rousseau-Nepton and the SIGNALS collaboration, which uses the SITELLE imaging spectrometer at the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope (CFHT) to observe ionized gas across nearby galaxies. I characterize fine-scale fluctuations in metallicity associated with spiral structure, studying how spiral arms trigger star formation and enrich the interstellar medium, with a focus on the galaxy NGC 628.

Dust Extinction in M33's HII Regions

I am the principal investigator of a CFHT/SITELLE program (Semester 25B), Mapping Dust Extinction in M33: A Multi-Wavelength Study of HII Regions with SITELLE, which uses dust as a lens on the structure and evolution of HII regions and how they are shaped by their galactic environment. By mapping dust extinction across HII regions throughout the nearby galaxy M33, I aim to connect dust properties to the physical processes that drive the evolution of the interstellar medium. As part of this project, I mentor an undergraduate research assistant on the data reduction and analysis.

Past Work

Novae and Nova Super-Remnants with the Condor Array Telescope

From 2022 to 2023, I worked with Dr. Michael Shara at the American Museum of Natural History alongside the Condor Array Telescope collaboration on two main efforts. First, I developed an automated pipeline to detect, measure, and classify transients in multi-epoch images and line-emission sources in multi-wavelength images, with the goal of identifying extragalactic novae.

Second, I used deep Condor imaging to study faint, low-surface-brightness nova remnants. I measured the expansion of the shell surrounding the dwarf nova Z Camelopardalis (pictured) by comparing Condor images with archival data, and analyzed SALT spectra of the newly discovered nova super-remnants surrounding KT Eridani and RS Ophiuchi.

This work contributed to papers on the Z Cam shell and the super-remnants around KT Eridani, RS Ophiuchi, and T Coronae Borealis.

Haverford College

From 2019 to 2022, I worked with Professors Karen Masters and Daniel Grin on two projects attempting to better understand how galaxies evolve and what they can tell us about how the universe behaves.

Tidally Induced Bars

For my senior thesis, The Interplay of Tides, Bars, and Star Formation in Disk Galaxies, I used Galaxy Zoo citizen science data to study how tidal interactions trigger or destroy bars in disk galaxies, combining morphological, environmental, and star formation measures. My poster on this work at the 240th meeting of the American Astronomical Society (June 2022) received an honorable mention for the Chambliss Astronomy Achievement Award; the poster can be found here and its abstract here.

Axion Dark Matter

I developed mock survey code to generate populations of dark matter haloes under different cosmologies and observe them with simulated HI surveys, to test whether observations of low-mass galaxies can constrain ultra-light axion dark matter. This work is published in MNRAS. My earlier poster on the project from the 237th meeting of the American Astronomical Society (January 2021) can be found here and its abstract here.
