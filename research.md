<h2>Interests</h2>
<p>Most broadly, I am interested in observational astronomy, both for its own research products as well as its use as a test of computational and theoretical predictions.</p>

<p>I am currently an astrophysics PhD student at the University of Toronto.</p>

<p>For a more comprehensive review of my work and experience, please see my <a href="{% link assets/images/CV.pdf %}" target="_blank">CV</a>.</p>

<h2>Past Work</h2>

<!-- Condor / AMNH -->

<h3>Novae and Cataclysmic Variables with the Condor Array Telescope</h3>

<p>Before starting my PhD, I worked with Dr. Michael Shara at the American Museum of Natural History alongside the <a href="https://condorarraytelescope.org/">Condor Array Telescope</a> collaboration, identifying interesting narrowband transients with a focus on cataclysmic variables. I also used the shell surrounding a dwarf nova to investigate classical nova ejection velocities and eruption timescales.</p>

<h3>Haverford College</h3>

<p>At Haverford, I focused on two projects attempting to better understand how galaxies evolve and what they can tell us about how the universe behaves. This work, done with Professors Karen Masters and Daniel Grin, gave me a particular interest in extragalactic astronomy, especially in the optical and radio regimes.</p>
